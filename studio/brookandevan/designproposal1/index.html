Structural changes

Three places where this proposal differs from the plans the architect has drawn. Each one needs to be noted on the plans and reviewed by the architect and the engineer.

02
  • Living room

    Keep the original living room wall

    The hall and the washer and dryer are removed, so the living room wall can stay where it is today instead of moving to the position drawn on the plans.

    Keeping the wall also keeps the original wood floor in the living room. Because this uses the wall that is already there, I would expect it to be simple on the engineering side.

Living room

Two proposals for the living room: the floor, and a focal point for the tall wall above the archway.

03

Floor

Light, sun-bleached wood in the living room, pale limestone-look tile in the kitchen, and a soft curved line where the two meet under the archway. The tile carries on outside so the kitchen and the outdoors read as one floor.

Living room

Keep the wood floor and refinish it lighter

Keeping the original living room wall means the original wood floor can stay. It is refinished to a much lighter tone, closer to the sun-bleached, boho and Mediterranean feel of the house.

Under the archway

A curved line, like a wave

The wood does not stop at a straight threshold. It ends in a circular line under the arch, where the floor turns into tile.

Kitchen and outside

Limestone-look tile, inside and out

Large 24 x 48 porcelain tile for the kitchen floor. The same colour continues outside as a thicker paver with more grip.

References

Focal point

The tall wall above the archway, under the ceiling, is where the eye lands in the living room. It needs one focal point. Three choices are recommended, each mocked up below.

  • Choice 1

    A lighting fixture

    A cluster of woven pendants hung at different heights from one point, filling the height of the room in front of the wall.

  • Choice 2

    A large piece of art

    One large piece on the wall above the archway.

  • Choice 3

    A textured wall

    A limestone wash or a solid colour on the whole wall, floor to ceiling with no break, over the archway and down both sides, so the surface itself is the feature.

These are quick mock-ups on the model view to compare the three ideas. They show the idea, not final selections.

Notes on the kitchen layout. More will be added here as the kitchen is worked out.

04
  • Island

    4' between the island and the counter

    The usual distance between an island and the counter is 3' to 4'. This plan uses 4', the generous end of that range.

    This is not fixed. The gap can come in toward 3' if more room is needed elsewhere in the kitchen.

Heating and cooling

A mini split heats and cools without ducts. Each room it serves gets its own indoor unit and its own control, so it can be added one room at a time or across the house. Three versions, from smallest to largest.
