Living Spaces

The living areas lean into open, light-filled volumes: vaulted wood-beam ceilings, deep-set window nooks, and a sunken lounge create a relaxed, gather-around feel rather than a formal parlor. Furniture stays low and casual — floor cushions, linen sofas, cane and rattan seating — so the architecture itself (plaster walls, exposed beams, arched openings) stays the visual anchor. A few gallery walls and coastal art keep the rooms personal without competing with the textures.

9 references

Kitchen

Kitchens favor a working, communal layout: islands and long wood tables anchor the room, with built-in banquette seating replacing a standalone breakfast table in several references. Cabinetry is warm wood rather than painted white, often left open-shelved, and arched niches or vaulted, skylit ceilings echo the same Mediterranean language used in the living spaces. Breakfast nooks are treated as an extension of the kitchen, not a separate room.

8 references

Bathroom

Bathrooms are the most tile-forward rooms in the set — green and sage zellige, arched tub surrounds, and a glass-roofed indoor-garden shower recur again and again. Fixtures are warm brass or matte black, vanities are unfinished or lightly stained wood, and several references blur the line between bathroom and greenhouse with abundant planting.

Mediterranean / Old-World Arches

Arched doorways, niches, and hand-troweled plaster define this group — a Spanish and Mediterranean vocabulary translated into a California context. Expect barrel-vaulted ceilings, warm stucco, and stone or terracotta-toned flooring throughout.

5 references

California Coastal / Boho

A looser, sun-bleached take on the same palette: linen and cane furniture, jute rugs, gallery walls of coastal art, and floor-level lounging. This is the most casual, livable style thread in the board — comfort-forward rather than precious.

6 references

Modern Rustic / Farmhouse

Wood beams and long communal tables reappear here, but with cleaner, more contemporary detailing — glass-paned cabinetry, matte black hardware, open shelving. This thread bridges the Mediterranean warmth with a more Scandinavian, pared-back restraint.

6 references

Minimalist Spa / Warm Neutral

The quietest style thread in the board: pared-back plaster boxes, minimal hardware, and a monochrome sand-and-stone palette. Bathrooms dominate this group, but the same restraint shows up in a couple of the living spaces too.

Zellige & Handmade Tile

Small-format, glazed zellige tile — mostly in sage and olive green — is the signature material of the bathrooms, laid across walls, floors, and arched tub surrounds alike. Its irregular, handmade texture is doing a lot of the “old-world” work in this board.

4 references

Warm Wood & Woven Natural Fiber

The largest material group: wood beams, cabinetry, and flooring paired with rattan, cane, and jute. This combination shows up in nearly every room type and is the connective tissue across the whole board.

12 references

Plaster, Stone & Stucco

Hand-troweled plaster walls, stone or concrete flooring, and stucco arches give the board its warm, monolithic backdrop — the “shell” that the wood and tile then get layered onto.
