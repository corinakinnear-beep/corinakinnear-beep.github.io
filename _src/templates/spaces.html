What I do

A permit set says where the walls go. It rarely says which way the light falls at four in the afternoon, or whether two people can pass each other at the door. That is my part.

Three deliverables

Design intent, room by room

Lighting, outlets, switching and dimming zones, finishes, fixtures, design choices, and the open questions for the architect — written so a project manager can hand it straight to a drafter.

Layout and circulation notes

Markups on the plans as they stand, in the terms the drafter needs. Where people enter, gather and get stuck. Small moves, named precisely, early enough to cost nothing.

Finish and lighting specification

Paint, surfaces, metals, cabinetry, colour temperature, CRI, dimming, how a strip is detailed so it doesn't read as a plastic extrusion. Specified to be bought and installed, not moodboarded.

Two projects

Garage conversion & ADU
Venice, Los Angeles

2026 · In permitting

A two-storey garage conversion and ADU. I wrote the design intent, and the permit set was drawn from it.

The design-intent document is organised room by room — lighting, outlets, switching and dimming zones, finishes, design choices, and the questions the architect needed to answer. The design-build firm's drafter worked inside that document rather than beside it, resolving specifics against my lines as the set came together.

  • Specified the palette: moss green and a warm white, waterproof microcement in a warm limestone beige through both bathrooms, burnished brass sealed.
  • Specified the lighting to buyable detail — everything on dimmers, 2" recessed 3000K downlights in matt white trim, 95+ CRI at the mirrors, LED strip mudded into a recessed channel rather than surface-mounted.
  • Marked up each plan revision in the drafter's terms: an extra foot towards the house, a ribbon window in the lower bathroom, the toilet and sink switched.
  • Caught a dimension error on site — the plans assumed seven feet between the neighbouring house and the existing garage. Measured, it was six. Found before the set was priced.

Studio Brava
Doha, Qatar

2018 · Completed

They were building a dance school and they had an architect. What they did not have was anyone who knew what happens in the room.

I was brought in on the general layout plan and the engagement ran well past it. I proposed a rectangular studio configuration and they built it; I flagged that the Studio 1 door sat close enough to the reception desk to jam traffic, which is the kind of thing that only shows up if you picture forty people arriving at once.

  • Reworked the studio geometry on the architect's layout plan.
  • Found the circulation conflict between the studio door and reception.
  • Sourced and assessed instructors, and recommended the teacher with regional experience over the stronger dancer, reasoning from the client's own oversight burden.
  • Specified what a studio needs to open: dancewear and shoe suppliers, a route through liability, a read on their branding options.
We ended up going with your idea to have a rectangular studio space. Fiorella Otero · Studio Brava · June 2018

How it works

The work

Best before the permit set is finalised, when a layout change is still a conversation rather than a revision fee. But useful at any point where there are drawings and the question of what the space is for is still open.

On a live project I'm on the weekly call, I turn edits around in a day or two, and I hand over documents the team can act on without translating them first.

Most of my working life has been spent putting bodies in rooms — as a dancer, choreographer and director. I read a floor plan the way I read a stage. It is the same skill.

What I don't do

I'm not the architect of record. The firm stays the firm. I'll recommend the drafters, contractors and suppliers I've vetted, and sit in the meetings.
